SPINAL INJURY — FIELD MANAGEMENT

WHEN TO SUSPECT
- High-speed trauma (fall from height, vehicle accident, diving into shallow water).
- Direct blow to spine or neck.
- Person complains of neck/back pain after trauma.
- Any tingling, numbness, or weakness in limbs after injury.
- All unconscious trauma victims — assume spinal injury until proven otherwise.

DO NOT MOVE UNLESS LIFE IS THREATENED
- If breathing but not in immediate danger: keep in position found.
- Support the head in neutral alignment with both hands.
- Do NOT twist, flex, or extend the spine.

IMMOBILISATION (FIELD COLLAR)
- Improvise collar: rolled newspaper, foam, clothing, towels around neck.
- Secure firmly but not tight enough to restrict breathing.
- Pad around the head with clothing on both sides to prevent rolling.

AIRWAY MANAGEMENT (IF UNCONSCIOUS)
- Jaw thrust method: push the jaw forward WITHOUT tilting the head.
- Only tilt head if jaw thrust fails to open airway — airway always takes priority.
- Recovery position if vomiting: log roll with minimum 3 people maintaining spine alignment.

IF PERSON MUST BE MOVED
- Log roll with at least 3 people: one person holds head, others on body.
- All rotate as a single unit on count.

MONITOR EVERY 5 MINUTES
- Breathing, pulse, movement/sensation in hands and feet.
- Any deterioration: means worsening cord injury. Keep still.

Medical care with imaging is required to confirm and treat spinal injuries.
