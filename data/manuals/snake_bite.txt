SNAKEBITE — FIELD MANAGEMENT

PRIORITY: IMMOBILISE AND CALM
- Keep the person completely still. Movement speeds venom spread.
- Lay flat. Keep affected limb BELOW heart level.
- Remove tight clothing, watches, rings near bite — swelling will make removal impossible.
- Cover bite with a clean dry dressing. Do not clean or manipulate wound.

DO NOT
- Do NOT cut the bite or try to suck the venom.
- Do NOT apply a tourniquet (worsens local tissue damage).
- Do NOT apply ice.
- Do NOT give alcohol, aspirin, or ibuprofen (thin blood).

PRESSURE IMMOBILISATION (FOR NEUROTOXIC SNAKES ONLY — e.g. cobras, mambas)
- Wrap the entire bitten limb in a firm crepe bandage from fingers/toes upward.
- Firm as for a sprained ankle — not a tourniquet.
- Immobilise with splint.
- Only use if envenomation known to cause nerve symptoms (drooping eyelids, weakness).
- Do NOT use for cytotoxic (tissue-destroying) bites: adders, puff adders.

SIGNS OF VENOM (MONITOR CLOSELY)
- Local: swelling, bruising, blistering (cytotoxic venom).
- Systemic: drowsiness, confusion, muscle weakness, drooping eyelids, difficulty breathing, bleeding from gums (haemotoxic).

IF BREATHING FAILS: Start CPR immediately.

Note the snake's appearance if safe to do so (colour, pattern, head shape). Antivenom choice depends on snake species. Seek medical care as soon as reachable.
