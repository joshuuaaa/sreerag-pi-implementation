DROWNING / NEAR-DROWNING — FIELD MANAGEMENT

SCENE SAFETY FIRST
- Do NOT enter moving or deep water unless trained.
- Reach with a rope, stick, clothing, or throw a floatation object to pull them in.
- Only enter shallow still water as last resort.

IMMANENT RESCUE
1. Remove from water immediately, supporting head and neck if fall/dive is suspected.
2. Call for help while working.

NOT BREATHING
- Start rescue breaths first (unlike cardiac arrest) — drowning victims need oxygen.
- 5 initial rescue breaths: tilt head back, pinch nose, full breath in over 1 second.
- If no pulse: begin full CPR (30 compressions : 2 breaths).
- Continue until breathing resumes or help arrives.

BREATHING
- Turn on their side (recovery position).
- Keep airway tilted open.
- Keep them warm — drowning causes rapid heat loss.
- Remove wet clothing and insulate with blankets or clothing.

CRITICAL WATCH-OUTS
- Breathing can worsen in the 4–6 hours after rescue (secondary drowning).
- Confusion, worsening cough, or laboured breathing = deteriorating.
- Any unconscious drowning victim needs medical care as soon as reachable.
- Suspect spinal injury if dived into shallow water — minimise neck movement.
