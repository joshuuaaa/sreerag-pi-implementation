PELVIC INJURY — FIELD MANAGEMENT

WHY DANGEROUS
- Unstable pelvic fractures can cause massive internal blood loss (3–5 litres).
- Shock develops rapidly and can be fatal within minutes.

IMMANENT ACTIONS
1. Keep the person completely flat and still.
2. Do NOT allow standing, walking, or repositioning of the legs.
3. Apply a PELVIC BINDER immediately:
   - If commercial binder not available: fold a sheet, belt, or clothing strip.
   - Wrap firmly around the hips (greater trochanters), not the abdomen.
   - Tie/secure as tight as tolerated.
   - This compresses the fractured pelvis and reduces bleeding space.

SHOCK MANAGEMENT (EXPECT SHOCK)
- Keep legs in the position found — do NOT elevate legs for pelvic fractures.
- Keep warm and insulated from the ground.
- Do NOT give food or drink.
- Monitor breathing, pulse, and skin colour every 5 minutes.
- Talk calmly to maintain consciousness.

SIGNS OF DETERIORATION
- Pale, cold, clammy skin + confusion + rapid breathing = decompensating shock.
- Send for any available help urgently.
- If breathing stops: CPR.

Additional care is required as soon as medically reachable — internal bleeding cannot be treated in the field.
