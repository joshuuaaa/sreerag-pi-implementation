CHEST INJURY — FIELD MANAGEMENT

TYPES
1. Rib fractures: sharp chest pain, worse on breathing. Sit upright at 45 degrees.
2. Open chest wound (sucking chest wound): air entering chest through wound.
3. Tension pneumothorax: collapsed lung. Signs: severe breathing difficulty, trachea shifts to uninjured side, absent breath sounds one side.
4. Flail chest: multiple rib fractures causing section of chest to move opposite to normal breathing.

OPEN CHEST WOUND
- Cover with non-porous material (plastic wrap, clean plastic bag, cling film).
- Tape THREE sides only. Leave fourth side open to allow air to escape.
- If patient worsens after sealing: lift corner of dressing to release pressure.

BREATHING POSITION
- Conscious: sit upright or lean toward injured side.
- Unconscious and breathing: recovery position on injured side.

CRITICAL SIGNS (DETERIORATING FAST)
- Rapid worsening breathing difficulty.
- Neck veins bulging, trachea shifted, no breath sounds one side = tension pneumothorax.
- Tracheal deviation: lift unsealed side of dressing immediately to release trapped air.

MONITOR EVERY 2 MINUTES
- Breathing rate, quality, skin colour.
- Level of consciousness.
- If breathing stops: CPR.

Seek medical care as soon as reachable. Rib fractures with breathing difficulty are serious.
