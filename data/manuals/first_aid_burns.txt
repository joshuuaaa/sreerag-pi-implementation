BURNS – FIRST AID REFERENCE

OVERVIEW
Burns are tissue injuries caused by heat, chemicals, electricity, radiation, or friction. Correct first aid prevents progression of tissue damage, reduces pain, and decreases infection risk. Serious burns require emergency medical care.

BURN DEPTH CLASSIFICATION

First-Degree Burns (Superficial)
Affects only outermost skin layer (epidermis).
Appearance: Red, dry, no blisters. Example: mild sunburn.
Pain: Moderate to severe surface pain.
Healing: 3–5 days without scarring.
First aid: Cool water, moisturiser. No emergency care needed.

Second-Degree Burns (Partial Thickness)
Affects epidermis and part of dermis.
Appearance: Red, wet, shiny, BLISTERS present. Very painful.
Healing: 2–3 weeks. May scar.
First aid: Cool water, non-stick dressing. Seek medical care if larger than palm of hand or on face/hands/genitals.

Third-Degree Burns (Full Thickness) – EMERGENCY
Entire skin destroyed. May extend to muscle or bone.
Appearance: Leathery, white, brown, or charred. Dry. May look waxy.
Pain: Paradoxically may feel LESS pain in center (nerve destruction). Very painful at edges.
Always requires emergency medical care. Never treat at home.

Fourth-Degree Burns
Extends through skin to muscle, bone, or tendons.
Always life-threatening. Call 911 immediately.

CRITICAL BURN CRITERIA – CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY
Call emergency services for any of the following:
- Burns covering more than 10% of body surface area (larger than 10 palms)
- Third-degree burns of ANY size
- Burns to FACE, HANDS, FEET, GENITALS, groin, or major joints
- Burns that circle completely around a limb, neck, or torso (circumferential)
- INHALATION INJURY: singed eyebrows or nostril hairs, hoarse voice, soot around mouth or nose, difficulty breathing, coughing
- Burns from CHEMICALS or ELECTRICITY
- Burns in children under 5 or adults over 60
- Burns in person with diabetes, immune deficiency, or other chronic illness
- Any burn that you're uncertain about

FIRST AID FOR THERMAL BURNS (heat, flame, scalding liquid)

STEP 1 – STOP THE BURNING PROCESS
Remove person from heat source. Call 911 for serious burns.
Remove burning or smouldering clothing (cut away if needed, do NOT pull over head if burning).
Remove jewellery and watches from burned area – swelling will make them impossible to remove later.

STEP 2 – COOL THE BURN
Run COOL (not cold) running water over the burn for at least 20 minutes.
Start cooling as soon as possible. Cooling within 3 hours is beneficial.
Do NOT use ice, ice water, or ice packs – this causes vasoconstriction and worsens damage.
Do NOT use butter, oil, toothpaste, egg white, or any home remedies – these trap heat and cause infection.
Do NOT pop blisters – blisters are a natural protective barrier against infection.

STEP 3 – COVER
After cooling: cover loosely with a sterile non-stick dressing or clean cling wrap (used lengthwise, not wrapped tightly around).
Do NOT use fluffy cotton wool – fibres stick to wound.
Do NOT wrap tightly – limbs may swell.
If cling wrap not available: a clean plastic bag over a hand or foot works well.

STEP 4 – MONITOR AND REASSURE
Watch for signs of shock (pale, cold skin, rapid breathing, confusion).
Keep person warm – burns cause rapid heat loss and hypothermia.
Lay person down with legs raised if showing signs of shock (unless burn is on legs/feet).
Do NOT give food or drink to person with large burns (may require surgery).
Small burns on alert person: allow sips of water if thirsty.

PAIN MANAGEMENT
Over-the-counter ibuprofen or paracetamol can help minor burns.
Do NOT apply topical anesthetics without medical advice.

CHEMICAL BURNS
1. Ensure YOUR safety first – wear gloves if available.
2. Remove contaminated clothing carefully (avoid spreading chemical to unaffected areas).
3. Flush affected area with large volumes of cool water for at least 20 minutes.
4. For dry chemicals: brush off powder before flushing.
5. Do NOT try to neutralise acids with alkali or vice versa – this generates heat.
6. For eye exposure: flush with water for 20 minutes, keeping eye open.
7. Seek emergency care for all chemical burns.
8. If known chemical: contact Poison Control or provide name to emergency services.

ELECTRICAL BURNS
CAUTION: Do NOT touch victim if still in contact with electricity source.
Turn off power at source before touching victim.
Electrical burns have small entry and exit wounds but massive internal damage along the conduction path.
Always call 911 for electrical burns – internal injuries may not be visible.
Person may have cardiac arrest from electrical shock – be prepared to perform CPR.

SUNBURN FIRST AID
Cool compresses or cool bath for 15 minutes.
Aloe vera gel or moisturising lotion (not butter or oil).
Ibuprofen or aspirin for pain and inflammation.
Hydrate with water.
Seek care for: blistering over large area, fever, chills, severe pain, or in children under 1 year.

SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS

Burns in Children
Children have thinner skin – the same exposure causes deeper burns in children than adults.
Children dehydrate and lose heat faster.
Any burn in a child that is larger than the child's palm requires medical evaluation.
Suspect non-accidental injury if burn pattern is unusual (stocking distribution, symmetric injuries).

Burns Near Airway
Signs of inhalation injury: hoarse voice, stridor (high-pitched breath sounds), burns inside mouth, singed nasal hairs, facial burns, coughing and wheezing.
This is a LIFE-THREATENING emergency – airway swelling can develop rapidly causing complete obstruction.
Call 911 immediately. Keep person upright if conscious.

DO NOT REMOVE
Do NOT remove stuck clothing, rings, or watches from burned areas (swelling).
Exception: Remove immediately if material is still burning or is caustic.

WOUND CARE FOR MINOR BURNS (after cooling)
1. Clean gently with mild soap and water after cooling.
2. Apply thin layer of antibiotic ointment (bacitracin or similar).
3. Cover with non-stick gauze dressing.
4. Change dressing daily.
5. Watch for infection: increasing pain, redness spreading beyond burn edge, swelling, pus, fever.
