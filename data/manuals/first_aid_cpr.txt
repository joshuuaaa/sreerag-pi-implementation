CPR AND CARDIAC ARREST – FIRST AID REFERENCE

OVERVIEW
Cardiac arrest occurs when the heart stops pumping effectively. Brain damage begins within 4 minutes without oxygen. CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation) maintains blood flow artificially until the heart can be restarted. Every minute without CPR reduces survival by 7–10%.

RECOGNISING CARDIAC ARREST
- Person is unresponsive (does not react to voice or touch)
- No normal breathing (only gasping counts as absent breathing)
- No pulse detectable (check carotid artery on neck for no more than 10 seconds)
- Pale, blue, or gray skin coloring
- Pupils may be fixed and dilated

CHAIN OF SURVIVAL
1. Recognise and call for help (call 911 immediately or have bystander call)
2. Begin CPR immediately
3. Use AED (defibrillator) as soon as available
4. Advanced medical care on arrival

ADULT CPR PROCEDURE (ages 8 and older)

STEP 1 – SCENE SAFETY
Check that the scene is safe before approaching. Do not become a second victim.

STEP 2 – CHECK RESPONSIVENESS
Tap shoulders firmly and shout "Are you okay?" twice. If no response, call 911.

STEP 3 – POSITION
Place person on their back on a firm, flat surface.
Kneel beside their chest.

STEP 4 – CHEST COMPRESSIONS
Position: Place heel of one hand on CENTER of chest, on the lower half of the breastbone (sternum). Place other hand on top, fingers interlaced, fingers lifted off chest.
Depth: Compress AT LEAST 2 inches (5 cm) but no more than 2.4 inches.
Rate: 100–120 compressions per minute. Use the beat of "Stayin' Alive" as a guide.
Recoil: Allow FULL chest recoil between compressions. Do not lean on chest.
Minimize interruptions: Keep pauses under 10 seconds.

STEP 5 – RESCUE BREATHS (if trained and willing)
Tilt head back, lift chin.
Pinch nose shut.
Create a seal over mouth.
Give breath over 1 second – watch for chest rise.
Give 2 breaths then immediately resume compressions.
RATIO: 30 compressions : 2 breaths.

COMPRESSION-ONLY CPR
If untrained or unwilling to give breaths: DO compression-only CPR.
Continuous compressions at 100–120/min are nearly as effective as 30:2 for the first few minutes and are better than nothing.

STEP 6 – CONTINUE
Continue until: AED arrives, trained help takes over, person shows obvious signs of life (breathing, coughing, movement), or you are physically unable to continue.

AED USE
1. Turn on AED – follow voice prompts.
2. Expose chest, attach pads as shown in diagram on pads.
3. Ensure no one is touching patient when AED analyzes.
4. If shock advised: shout "Clear!" ensure no contact, press shock button.
5. Immediately resume CPR starting with compressions.
6. Repeat every 2 minutes.

CHILD CPR (Ages 1–8)
Same as adult with modifications:
- Depth: 2 inches (one-third of chest depth)
- Use one or two hands depending on child's size
- Give 30 compressions : 2 breaths
- Tilt head less aggressively for airway
- If alone: give 2 minutes of CPR BEFORE calling 911 (different from adults)

INFANT CPR (Under 1 year)
1. Place on firm flat surface.
2. Check response: tap foot, do not shake.
3. Compressions: Two fingers on CENTER of chest just below nipple line.
4. Depth: 1.5 inches (1/3 of chest depth).
5. Rate: 100–120 compressions per minute.
6. Rescue breaths: Cover both mouth AND nose with your mouth. Give gentle puff (just enough to see chest rise). 30:2 ratio.
7. If alone: 2 minutes CPR before calling 911.

CHOKING – ADULT AND CHILD (over 1 year)

MILD CHOKING (can cough, speak, or breathe)
Encourage strong, forceful coughing. Do NOT interfere. Do NOT perform back blows or abdominal thrusts – this may make it worse. Stay with person and reassure.

SEVERE CHOKING (cannot breathe, speak, or cough; universal choking sign – hands at throat)
STEP 1 – CALL 911 immediately or have bystander call.
STEP 2 – 5 BACK BLOWS: Lean person forward. Give 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades with heel of hand.
STEP 3 – 5 ABDOMINAL THRUSTS (HEIMLICH MANEUVER): Stand behind person. Make a fist above navel, below breastbone. Grasp fist with other hand. Pull sharply IN and UP. Repeat 5 times.
STEP 4 – ALTERNATE: Continue alternating 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until object dislodged or person loses consciousness.

UNCONSCIOUS CHOKING VICTIM
If person becomes unconscious: lower to floor, call 911, begin CPR.
Before giving rescue breaths: look in mouth for visible object. If seen and easily graspable, remove it. Do NOT perform blind finger sweeps.

INFANT CHOKING (Under 1 year)
Do NOT perform abdominal thrusts on infants.
STEP 1 – FACE-DOWN BACK BLOWS: Hold infant face-down on forearm, supporting head. Give 5 firm back blows between shoulder blades with 2 fingers.
STEP 2 – FACE-UP CHEST THRUSTS: Turn infant face-up. Give 5 chest thrusts with 2 fingers in center of chest (same position as infant CPR).
STEP 3 – Check mouth for object. Only remove if clearly visible.
STEP 4 – Alternate 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts until dislodged or infant becomes unresponsive.

RECOVERY POSITION
For an unconscious person who IS breathing normally:
1. Kneel beside person.
2. Place arm nearest you at right angle to body.
3. Place far arm across chest, hold back of their hand against their near cheek.
4. Pull up far knee, keeping foot on ground.
5. Pull on bent knee to roll person toward you onto their side.
6. Tilt head back slightly to maintain airway.
7. Monitor breathing continuously until help arrives.
8. This prevents choking on vomit.

POST-RESUSCITATION CARE
Person who returns to breathing: Place in recovery position. Keep monitoring. Brief them gently if conscious. Do NOT let them get up or walk – cardiac events can recur. Wait for emergency services.

COMMON CPR MISTAKES TO AVOID
- Stopping compressions too often or for too long
- Compressing too shallow (less than 2 inches)
- Not allowing full chest recoil
- Tilting head angle too steep (compresses airway)
- Starting with rescue breaths instead of compressions
- Panicking – steady, rhythmic compressions are more important than perfection
